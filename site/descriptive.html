Pearson vs Spearman with hunger

Group-difference significance tests

Both tests are reported because ANOVA assumes normal, equal-variance groups and the hunger rate is skewed — Kruskal–Wallis makes no such assumption. They agree, so the group differences are not an artefact of the test chosen.

Part 3

Patterns across time and groups

The trends the models were built to learn from.

1The world was winning — then the shocks came

Hunger fell for a decade, then COVID-19 and the 2022 price shock erased much of the progress.

2Where hunger lives

Sub-Saharan Africa carries by far the highest burden, near 20%. It and the Middle East & North Africa are the only regions still above their 2010 level.

3Hunger follows income — structurally

In the poorest group the typical country sits above the serious-hunger line.

4Money matters — until it doesn't

Each dot is a country in the latest year. Income cuts hunger fast among the poor, then the effect flattens — the curvilinearity the rank correlation picks up.

5Importing food isn't the problem

Import dependence alone shows no clear hunger trend — a genuine null result.

6Tier composition over time

The share of countries in each tier, year by year — the shock years are visible as a rise in the high-risk band.
Part 4

Association rules (Apriori)

Combinations of conditions that occur together more often than chance. Lift is how many times more likely the pattern is than if the conditions were independent.

Import dependence looks harmless on its own. It raises risk only combined with low income — a pattern single-variable analysis cannot see, and the reason this unsupervised step earns its place alongside the models.
